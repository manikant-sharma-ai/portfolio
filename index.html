01 — About

I don't write the code.
I make sure it is right.

I'm an AI generalist. That is not a borrowed job title, it is what the work actually is: moving between prompt, context, loop and graph depending on what the job needs, and picking whichever free tool tests best for that job.

I started a year ago with ordinary chat. The first thing I built was my own agent: a scheduled worker that checks whether the job is actually done, creates follow-up tasks when it needs more time, wakes itself and keeps going, and writes notes into a GitHub repo so the next run starts with context instead of from zero. It ran on free models with no API key and cost me nothing.

I don't write code and I don't intend to. I direct it, and I took a Python course for one reason: so I can describe a job well enough that the AI cannot fill the gaps with guesses, read the structure it hands back, and catch its mistakes. A team leader who cannot do the work cannot get the work done. Same rule here.

For now I build for myself, not for clients. Feedfy is next: instead of scrolling for an hour to find content worth keeping, a feed that goes and finds it, in about two minutes.

01 · Prompt

Telling the AI exactly what you want

A role, the situation, the problem, and what you need back — the same four things you would give a person before asking them to act.

Open the library → 02 · Context

Showing before telling

A designer does not guess your style — you show the thumbnails you like and the ones that earned clicks. Context is handing that over before the work starts.

Open the library → 03 · Loop

Doing the same thing until the job is done

Ten boxes, one trip each, and you keep going until the last one is across. A loop stops on a condition you wrote down, not when the AI feels finished.

Open the library → 04 · Graph

More than one pair of hands

A writer and an examiner, with an order between them — and an examiner the writer cannot instruct. Break the job up, and keep the final call human.

06 — How I build

Five things I do before I trust anything an AI built.

Not gates for a launch, gates for my own conscience. Skip one and the work goes back in the drawer.

01

Describe the job until guessing is impossible

Role, situation, constraint, and exactly what should come back. If I cannot describe it, the AI fills the gap with invention, and the fault is mine, not the model's.

02

Hand over the material, not just the words

The thumbnails that got clicks, the real posts, the live numbers through a connector. Context is what stops it designing for a customer who does not exist.

03

Give it a loop with a stop condition

Repeat until the condition is met, not until it feels finished. Write the condition down before you start, or you will accept whatever comes back.

04

Put the examiner outside the loop

A separate step with its own rules, that the writer cannot instruct, and a human holding the final call. A writer that grades its own work grades itself generously.

05

Keep the notes

What worked goes back into the repo so the next run starts ahead of the last one, and nothing ships that I did not read myself.
